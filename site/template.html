Did evolution protect the Alzheimer's genes?

Three genes, APP, PSEN1 and PSEN2, cause rare inherited early-onset Alzheimer's disease. This project compares them across five mammals to see which parts never changed, and whether known disease mutations hit those parts.

Three genes, one job: making amyloid-beta

APP 770 amino acids

The precursor protein. Two cuts release amyloid-beta, the sticky piece that builds up as plaques in Alzheimer's brains.

PSEN1 467 amino acids

Presenilin-1, the cutting core of gamma-secretase. Its two key aspartates, positions 257 and 385, do the cutting.

PSEN2 448 amino acids

Presenilin-2, a close sibling of PSEN1 with the same job. Its key aspartates sit at 263 and 366.

What this can and can't tell you

Conserved is not the same as harmful

A mutation on a conserved letter is one clue. Whether it causes disease is decided by clinical evidence.

Not a diagnosis

This compares database sequences. It cannot predict anyone's personal risk.

Five species only

A small, fixed set chosen in advance. More species would sharpen the picture.

Databases change

ClinVar classifications get updated, so every record is pinned to its version and access date.
